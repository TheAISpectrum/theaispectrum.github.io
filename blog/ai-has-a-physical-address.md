The phrase “artificial intelligence” sounds weightless. Its physical reality is anything but. Every generated token depends on a chain of **chips, servers, networking equipment, cooling systems, power infrastructure, and buildings**.

## The invisible machine becomes visible

At the center are accelerators designed to perform huge numbers of parallel mathematical operations. Those chips sit inside servers; servers are grouped into racks; racks are connected through extremely fast networks; and the entire system must be powered and cooled continuously.

That stack matters because the capabilities people experience in a browser are constrained by physical decisions made far away from the interface.

## Datacenters are part of the product

Latency, cost, reliability, and scale are not purely software questions. A model can only serve millions of users if the surrounding infrastructure can move data quickly, distribute workloads, remove heat, and recover from failures.

> AI may be accessed through the cloud, but the cloud is still made of machines.

## Why this lens matters

Looking at AI physically changes the questions we ask. Instead of only asking what a model can do, we can ask **what it requires**, where those requirements are concentrated, and how engineering choices shape who can build and deploy powerful systems.
