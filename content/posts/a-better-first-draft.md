A blank page can turn a small task into a long one. AI can offer a starting point, but the presence of a fluent draft does not tell us whether the work is finished.

Here is a suggested workflow to try on a low-stakes task: turning your own notes into a meeting agenda. It is a practical experiment, not a promise of a particular time saving.

## Start with your own goal

Write down what the meeting must achieve before opening an AI tool. Decide who will read the agenda, what they already know, and which decisions need their attention.

That small act gives you criteria for judging the output.

## Give it a bounded job

Instead of asking the tool to “plan everything”, ask for a specific transformation of information you provide.

```text
Turn the notes below into a 30-minute meeting agenda.
Use only the information in the notes.
Separate discussion topics from decisions.
Flag missing details instead of inventing them.

Notes:
[Insert notes that are appropriate to share with the tool.]
```

A precise prompt can clarify your intent. It does not guarantee that the tool will follow every instruction.

## Review before you use it

Compare the agenda with your original notes. Check whether it added unsupported details, left out a concern, or allocated an unrealistic amount of time.

| Let the tool suggest | Keep responsibility for |
| --- | --- |
| A clearer order | The actual priorities |
| Shorter wording | Meaning and accuracy |
| Possible omissions | Whether those omissions matter |

## Count the whole task

For your next few attempts, compare the total time spent briefing, checking, and revising with the time you would normally spend doing the work yourself.

Also ask whether the result is useful. A faster agenda that directs everyone toward the wrong decision is not an improvement.

> Treat the draft as something to evaluate, not as evidence that the task is complete.

Your judgment is part of the workflow. Make room for it.
