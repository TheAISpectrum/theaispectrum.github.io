## The Zombie AI Phenomenon

Let's admit it, we all use AI. It has slowly crept into every crevice of our lives. From our vacation planning to our midnight assignments, AI has profoundly impacted our lives. And while the **efficiency increase is dramatic**, there is a darker, more sinister side.

I present the concept of a “Zombie AI User”. A “zombie” AI user is not someone who uses AI frequently. It is someone who **detaches themselves from the thinking process** while using AI. A zombie user gets a task, forwards it to AI, and forgets about it. Prompt. Receive. Accept. Repeat.

In this entire process, the zombie user has gained no new knowledge and receives the illusion of achievement. The work might look flawless. Everyone might love it. But they still haven’t worked. A zombie user’s nightmare is a world without AI. Not because it reduces their productivity like a regular user, but because they may **struggle to work independently**.

I am not accusing anyone of outsourcing their thinking. Not all zombie users become passive in the thinking process intentionally. Most are just regular AI users who have slowly drifted away from the task and just let AI act as a proxy. 

### When the Answer Replaces the Thinking
Do you remember the world before AI? I personally feel it was much more tranquil. If you ever received a task, you would actually have to sit and think about how to do it. The only form of plagiarism was blatantly copying from Wikipedia. And even when you copied from Wikipedia, you would learn something along the way. A random fun fact. A surprising connection to a movie you watched last month.

With AI, that entire process is compressed into the span of seconds. What used to take hours of searching through articles and venturing onto the forbidden second page of Google can now be done before you drink a sip of coffee. And that speed is what is causing the problem. There are only a few letters of difference between **“AI helps me think”** and **“AI thinks for me”**, and I fear we are transitioning towards the latter. 

I know there are people who will argue that this is just **another innovation**. We stopped solving equations by hand when calculators arrived. We stopped using compasses when GPS arrived. But AI is different. 1 + 1 and the route to Starbucks are tasks that do not require deep human thinking. Aliens won’t show up tomorrow morning and declare 1 + 1 = 3 (please wake me up if they do). But AI hits at something different. It allows us to **offload the reasoning, evaluation, and creativity** that make us human. 

Zombie users are not formed because of laziness or a lack of skill. It is a gradual process of slowly offloading this thinking where, before you know it, **AI has taken over your thinking**.

### Even the Data is Against Zombies
Studies from numerous researchers and institutes have shown that offloading thinking has real, measurable impacts on the human mind. A [study](https://www.ijfmr.com/research-paper.php?id=73184) conducted in Chennai, India by Ganesh et al. found a strong negative correlation between AI usage and critical thinking in 300 young adults. Generative-AI dependence showed a **−0.902 correlation with critical thinking and −0.958 with problem-solving**. Even if you do not have a background in statistics, these numbers are extremely strong negative correlations (for context, correlation ranges from −1 to +1: −1 represents a perfect negative correlation, 0 no linear correlation, and +1 a perfect positive correlation). While the correlation does exist, it is important we remember the scientific principle of **correlation ≠ causation**. The study shows an association, but does not prove that AI definitely caused the poorer thinking.

Moreover, a joint [study](https://www.google.com/url?q=https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/lee_2025_ai_critical_thinking_survey.pdf&sa=D&source=docs&ust=1789392555574391&usg=AOvVaw0Kqu5MsaPMaJTcVZJJcK1v) by researchers from Microsoft and Carnegie Mellon University by Lee et al. found that amongst 319 knowledge workers (i.e. people whose jobs require critical thinking and analysis), data showed that higher confidence in GenAI was associated with **less critical-thinking effort**, while **higher confidence in one's own abilities was associated with more**. Very simply, the more people trusted AI, the less critical-thinking effort they reported putting into the task.

A final study before you get bored and click off this blog. MIT conducted a [study](https://www.google.com/url?q=http://brainonllm.com&sa=D&source=docs&ust=1789393402942523&usg=AOvVaw1fWrHOv-3pCpvT9ymv8gq_) with 54 participants on essay writing tasks in different conditions. They used electroencephalography (EEG) technology to monitor electrical activity in the participants’ brains. Those who used purely their brain showed the most widely distributed neural connectivity. Those who used a search engine showed intermediate connectivity. And those with access to an LLM showed the **weakest overall**. Another interesting result was that people who initially wrote with an LLM still showed weaker neural connectivity afterwards when they did not have AI.

### Why Do Zombies Not Notice?
The problem with the zombie effect is that people may subconsciously start using AI to hide their weaknesses. With regular thinking, if I do not know something, at least I know that I don’t know it. However, with AI, the narrative shifts. Now, you can just ask AI to do it for you, and you appear to be all-knowing. You can write an entire history essay without knowing who Napoleon was. You can write a book without knowing the main character’s name.

So people may have become zombies when they initially started using AI to cover up what they didn’t know. It may or may not be intentional. I am not calling out zombies as “dumb” or “unskilled”. Everyone has their own expertise and not everyone needs to be a master of everything. Our diversity in skills is what makes us human.

As found in the Microsoft-Carnegie Mellon study, people with confidence in AI are less likely to engage critically.

> Being a zombie doesn't make you look less intelligent. It lets you look intelligent without requiring you to think.

### How Not to Become a Zombie (or How to Come Back From Zombieland)
If you felt like you had some zombie-esque traits, or if you know someone who is a potential zombie, please understand and share these steps:

1) **Think before prompting** — Before outsourcing your work, form your own opinions, ideas, or hypotheses about it.
2) **Interrogate the output** — Do not blindly accept the AI’s output. Question what it assumed and where it got its data from.
3) **Understanding is not production** — After receiving a produced answer and reading it, do not assume you have understood it. Try writing it in your own words. Or, try talking to a rubber duck—apparently it helps programmers debug their code better.
4) **Use friction deliberately** — Intentionally solve the problem yourself before asking AI to help out.
5) **Banter with AI** — When creating arguments or analysis, ask AI to play the Devil’s advocate and present counterarguments that eventually will help you improve the strength of your work.

In conclusion, becoming a zombie is becoming easier as AI becomes increasingly embedded in our lives. Again, I am not asking you to abandon or stop using AI; I simply ask for **a change in behaviour**. I hope you share my advice with zombies you may know, and if you yourself identify as a zombie after reading this blog, I hope I was able to help you come back from Zombieland.
